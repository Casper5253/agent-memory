# Risks and failure modes

This is an adversarial design review, not a claim that every risk has occurred. Severity reflects impact if the condition occurs. Test IDs are binding acceptance cases for the work packages. Fixtures must be synthetic and must not invoke real model tools or production hooks.

## Required design revisions from this review

The initial idea of treating Markdown as both editable authority and generated output was rejected. Structured state now has one authority, with edits imported through explicit review. The design accepts the cost of database backup rather than pretending approvals can be recovered from prose.

The initial idea of numeric confidence decay was rejected. Age becomes freshness. Acceptance, evidence level, and freshness remain separate fields. This avoids turning recent unsupported claims into higher-ranked truth.

The initial idea of global graph navigation was rejected. Version 1 has project-scoped edges, one-hop expansion, and no automatic entity merging. Cross-project references require a separately reviewed future design.

The initial idea of hooking synthesis directly into capture was rejected. A durable scan of completed captures queues knowledge work after the legacy cursor commits. Knowledge outages cannot erase capture progress.

## Residual limitations

Exact supporting text proves that a source made a statement, not that the statement is true. Natural-language entailment and contradiction remain fallible. The system labels this limitation rather than hiding it behind a score.

The single-user local permission model does not defend against an adversary who can edit the database or execute arbitrary commands as the user. Prompt-injection tests establish containment at the data/model boundary, not OS-level access separation.

Advisory locks cannot make Obsidian obey database transactions. Hash checks and preimage backups make conflicts detectable and recoverable, but a race with an uncooperative editor remains possible. Activation must demonstrate recovery under a controlled editor-race fixture.

Backups create a retention/privacy tradeoff. Ordinary retraction preserves evidence; privacy purge does not. Purge requires an explicit inventory of DB, WAL, backups, exports, pages, and QMD data, plus operator authorization. The release does not silently destroy immutable session records to satisfy an inferred retention policy.
